    release cycle

    MethodCI/CD owned end to end through Azure DevOps, with SSRS and Power BI release steps automated and Scrum aligning dev, test, IT, and business on shared priorities.

    ScopeRoughly 11 weeks of cross-team idle time removed per release. Client changes ship in weeks rather than seasons.

  2. Pipeline E1-CDC

    30 min → to under 8 min

    CDC ETL runtime

    MethodFull-table reloads replaced with idempotent incremental merge-upserts. A failed run reruns clean, with no manual cleanup first.

    ScopeCompute down 67%. Data moved falls from roughly 2.46M TB/yr to 257 TB/yr, with the data-freshness SLA held. Now the platform's standing CDC pattern.

  3. Provisioning E-IDEM

    1 hr, 20+ times a day → to one idempotent run

    database copy and restore

    MethodFour latent failure modes closed: orphaned CDC cleanup jobs terminated properly, not just dropped from metadata, a guard that kills active replication sessions before disabling CDC, silently-defaulting restore parameters fixed, and stuck-restore detection with pre-flight race checks.

    ScopeAn hour of manual orchestration per request, 20+ requests a day: 5,000 engineering hours a year, about $300K, or 2.5 engineers back on product. Safe retry replaces manual cleanup.

  4. Reporting E3-FABRIC

    12s → to under 4s

    query response

    MethodLegacy SQL Server reporting migrated onto Microsoft Fabric semantic models, with index and stored-procedure tuning on the OLAP paths. The gain came from the semantic layer and the tuning, not from the release tooling.

    ScopeClient support tickets down 40%, returning roughly $243K a year of analyst capacity. Support scales without adding headcount.

  5. Observability M2

    1:5 → to 1:1.2

    signal-to-noise on alerts

    MethodData observability with tunable per-service thresholds, so a threshold could be tightened for one service without going deaf on another.

    ScopeAbout 250 non-actionable P3 alerts filtered every week. Ended the alert blindness that was masking real incidents.

  6. Cost M3

    anomaly-detection compute

    MethodWorkloads migrated off static D-series VMs onto AKS with horizontal pod autoscaling, sized to real load instead of peak.

The CDC ETL reloaded whole tables every run, and standing up a database was an hour of manual orchestration, twenty-plus times a day. I moved CDC onto idempotent incremental merge-upserts and rewrote provisioning to be safely rerunnable, closing four latent failure modes on the way. ETL went 30 minutes to under 8 with compute down 67% and the freshness SLA intact; provisioning gave back 5,000 engineering hours a year, roughly $300K.

Streaming risk

Portfolio Risk Analytics

Overnight VaR means a portfolio manager who moves a position intraday has no idea what their risk looks like until morning. A Kafka producer streams market ticks, Spark Structured Streaming aggregates on 5-second tumbling windows behind a 10-second watermark, a Delta writer lands the bronze layer, and FastAPI computes historical-simulation VaR at 95% and 99% with Streamlit on top. Historical simulation over a 30-day window, chosen over a parametric model because equity returns are fat-tailed and a normal assumption understates the tail. Component VaR per position, so the dashboard names the holding driving the risk. On a fresh checkout the API falls back to a seeded sample set, worth knowing before reading a number off it.
